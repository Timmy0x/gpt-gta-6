# Underwater surface correction

The v11 normal-game WebGPU capture at `docs/evidence/coast-gameplay-webgpu-v11/underwater.png` shows a gray opaque ceiling and a pale horizon slit. Its camera is approximately `(258.109, −1.035, −34.984)`, 0.855m below the mean ocean surface, with underwater EXP2 fog density .095. The old isolated preview did not use that same camera depth, angle or underwater fog.

Inspection of the installed Babylon 9.25 native WaterMaterial found separate causes:

- The default shader blends transmission using `max(dot(view,+Y),0)`. Below the plane this becomes zero, leaving only its above-water reflection target.
- Both RTT clip planes and render lists still assume an above-water observer, so underwater reflection and refraction use the wrong half-spaces.
- The alternate native Fresnel expression raises the signed viewing dot product to a floating-point power before taking its absolute value. Negative bases are outside the defined `pow` domain in the [Khronos GLSL specification](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/gl/GLSLangSpec.4.60.html) and [W3C WGSL specification, §17.5.46](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#pow-builtin), inspected 2026-09-09.
- The atmospheric Sky deliberately ignores fog. Rays exposed beyond the finite water surface/far plane can therefore show a bright unattenuated sky line in a submerged view.

## Implementation

Ocean retains the installed WaterMaterial and its native shader infrastructure, moving normal field, mirror transform, two RTTs, light response and surface geometry. A before-camera observer runs after camera interpolation and before the material targets. Below the surface it selects submerged geometry for reflection and above-water geometry for refraction, then reverses the half-space clips through the targets' public observables. Native mirror transforms and native restore callbacks remain in use. Both target refresh counters reset at a medium change, avoiding a stale above-water capture for a refresh interval.

`OceanWaterMaterial.ts` subclasses native WaterMaterial and replaces only its separate Fresnel expression in a scoped effect variant. It obtains shader sources and interface names through public Effect accessors, leaving the global ShaderStore unchanged. The normal above-water branch is untouched. The underwater branch uses the unpolarized dielectric Fresnel equations with refractive index 1.333, including zero transmission beyond the water-to-air critical angle. It reads the actual bound camera position and animated native surface normal. It does not fold or relocate the eye, rewrite native readiness internals, or move the actual camera, world projection, mirror transform or fog distance. A changed pinned publisher expression fails explicitly. Two effect variants are cached across dry/wet transitions in the focused preview.

The initial native-only repair correctly selected both targets but retained the publisher's maximum .65 transmission weight. A near-zenith v2 diagnostic captured both RTTs and proved that refraction contained the blue sky and sun while reflection contained the seabed; the min35% reflected contribution caused the remaining ceiling. The scoped correction removes that cap and the signed floating-power domain problem. At normal incidence the transmitted weight is approximately .98; at grazing submerged angles the reflected seabed remains visible as total internal reflection, which is an intended interface behavior.

Main calls `ocean.setAtmosphericFog(density, color)` with the atmospheric values immediately before applying its underwater fog override. When submerged, the refraction RTT temporarily uses that air fog for above-water scenery and restores the main underwater fog afterwards. The water surface's own fog then accounts approximately for the eye-to-surface segment. The reflection RTT remains in the underwater medium. Clip-plane and fog restoration are independently tested.

`Underwater.ts` adds a depth-tested distant extinction box that uses the same scene fog as other submerged geometry. It is centred through Babylon's `infiniteDistance` transform, lies safely inside the camera far plane, writes no depth, and is excluded from ocean target lists. It fills effectively extinguished far-water rays that previously exposed an unfogged sky gap. Nearby seabed, objects and the real water surface remain in front of it; above-world geometry is still captured through refraction. It is disabled as soon as the camera leaves the water. The addition costs one mesh/material and 12 triangles, with no texture or render target.

## Limits and evidence

This corrects camera-side target selection, the dielectric angular blend and finite background gaps. Refraction remains a projected scene capture rather than bent Snell-law rays; the sky window's angular weighting is physical but objects viewed through it are not ray-traced or optically compressed. Scene EXP2 fog approximates extinction without wavelength-dependent absorption/scattering volumes, caustics or simulated light shafts. Native clip offsets remain ±5cm. A grazing underwater view still reflects the seabed; that is distinct from the rejected opaque zenith cap. These are bounded defect corrections, not a claim of finished real-water optics.

`node --import tsx --test tests/ocean.test.ts` passes five tests, including dielectric incidence/critical-angle behavior, exact pinned GLSL/WGSL extension and global ShaderStore isolation, camera-side target roles, clip signs, atmospheric-fog restoration, source caps and repeated disposal. The parent frozen v14 build passed its full 170-test suite with these three runtime files. The isolated source build also succeeded.

`docs/evidence/ocean-underwater-v1/result.json` records the first 24-stage isolated run, which was not visually accepted because of the remaining zenith cap. `ocean-underwater-v2` contains the targeted zenith and raw RTT diagnostic proving correct source content. The final `docs/evidence/ocean-underwater-v3/result.json` records 12 frozen-production stages, six each on actual WebGPU and WebGL2, with no shader/runtime errors. Both backends compiled the scoped variant and stabilized at two cached variants after resurfacing.

The inspected v3 images show sky/sun transmission near zenith, a clear angular sky window in the upward view, no bright far-water slit at the v11 camera pose, intact above-water shoreline reflections, and correct return to the dry surface branch. The shoreward grazing capture retains an internally reflected seabed. This isolated fixture uses simplified scale geometry and does not establish integrated map performance or final gameplay fidelity; the parent is separately testing the full-game v14 coast.
