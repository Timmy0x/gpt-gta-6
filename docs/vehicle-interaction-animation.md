# Contact-driven vehicle interaction animation

This checkpoint adds contact-driven animation over the verified civilian occupancy state machine. It retains the same driver entity, injury state, vehicle control lock, 0.32-second approach, 0.78-second driver withdrawal plus 0.08-second handoff, 0.65-second entry, and 0.65-second exit. It is an authored GTA V-style fallback, not a confirmed GTA VI animation or a finished captured grapple.

The entrant reaches toward the moving front-door handle, crouches toward the driver, contacts the near shoulder and forearm, shifts weight backward, and makes one short recovery step before releasing. The driver turns toward the opening, clears the seat, rises onto staggered feet and transfers to existing fleeing behavior. Entry delays hip lowering until the body clears the sill, uses an outside support foot and hand support, and returns to the previously tested final seating profile. Exit reverses this motion and uses the actual selected exit side.

`CharacterContactIK.ts` adapts the installed Babylon 9.25 `BoneIKController` to the gameplay skeleton's downward rest axes. Direct application to those axes aimed away from the target. A private positive-Y solver rig solves the two segments; the resulting directions rotate the original gameplay bones. The existing Rocketbox observer retargets the licensed visual skin from those bones. A single shared-root solver skeleton holds at most thirteen bones, is created only when needed, adds no render meshes or physics bodies, and is disposed with the Character. The shared root is required because this installed Skeleton implementation evaluates absolute matrices from its first root.

`Character.jointPosition`, `reachHand`, `plantFoot` and `interactionPosture` expose the contact operations. Dead and ragdoll guards prevent those overlays from replacing a casualty pose. Foot targets move through the step while the supporting IK solve remains active: fading the solve itself during a crouched release caused a real visual shoe penetration, which was corrected.

## CPU verification

`node --import tsx --test tests/vehicle-interaction-pose.test.ts tests/vehicle-occupancy.test.ts tests/movement.test.ts tests/aircraft-seating.test.ts`

All fifteen tests passed. They cover existing Havok occupancy/blocked exits/control locks/identity preservation, aircraft seat containment, native IK accuracy under translated and rotated roots, reach clamping without bone stretching, resource cleanup, casualty guards and continuous root paths.

The contact tests exercise eight combinations: Jason and Lucia, both licensed civilian models, and the sedan and detailed concept car. Across the grasp interval the largest wrist-to-shoulder/forearm target gap was 0.04127 m; the support ankle drift was less than 0.0000001 m. They sampled 69,456 vertices from the actual licensed protagonist skins, all finite and within the expected body extent; the lowest sampled point was 0.02646 m above the street. These are sampled vertex and joint checks, not exhaustive collision checks for every animation frame or every visual vertex. Fingers retain the source model's rest articulation; a wrist target does not prove a closed-finger grip.

The frozen coastal-v13 candidate completed normal-control WebGPU and WebGL2 runs in `docs/evidence/vehicle-interaction-v13-{webgpu,webgl}`: recorded sixteen and seventeen checkpoints respectively with zero page errors or console warnings. Both used the SUV and retained normal traffic, walking, E theft, held W during transitions, character switching, driving and dismount. These establish control/identity behavior, **not animation acceptance**. Review found the actors stand too close and the pull reads as a clasp rather than a convincing extraction. Sparse screenshots also miss intermediate foot and doorway motion. A consecutive 60 Hz frame review of both protagonists with the sedan and detailed concept model is required before accepting the choreography. Earlier `vehicle-occupancy-v10-*` screenshots validate the previous state-machine checkpoint, not these new contact poses. Remaining visual gaps include authored rather than captured movement, finger grip/handle articulation, long-garment behavior, and broader vehicle/body-shape coverage. No frame-rate or long-session performance result is claimed.
