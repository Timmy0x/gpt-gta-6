# Procedural coastal sky

`src/core/Sky.ts` wraps Babylon's native `SkyMaterial` from the exact `@babylonjs/materials@9.25.0` package. The package declares compatibility with core 9.x and resolves to the project's same core 9.25.0. Its pinned implementation includes both GLSL and native WGSL shaders. The [official material documentation](https://doc.babylonjs.com/toolsAndResources/assetLibraries/materialsLibrary/skyMat/) and [publisher source](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Babylon.js/blob/master/packages/dev/materials/src/sky/skyMaterial.ts) were inspected; actual compatibility and shader behavior were checked against the installed 9.25.0 files, rather than assuming current master matches the lockfile.

The native shader supplies Rayleigh/Mie scattering, horizon haze, a solar disk, dusk coloration and dithering without a downloaded sky texture. Clear, Haze and Rain select explicit turbidity and particle-scattering defaults. A fixed seeded 160-star hemisphere fades in during twilight and disappears in rain. Stars are original authored positions, not a real astronomical catalog.

The clock drives an authored 06:00 sunrise / 18:00 sunset arc, with a southward noon tilt. This keeps the solar disk and directional lighting on the same vector. It is an equinox-like project decision, not a claim about Leonida latitude, season, or Rockstar's simulation. The returned horizon color is a deliberately restrained fog palette, not an exact sample of the scattering shader at every viewing azimuth.

## Integration

Construct `new Sky(scene)` immediately after `new Scene(engine)`, before constructing any world materials. Babylon's default opaque queue sorts by material creation ID: the sky's depth-write-free background needs to render before opaque world geometry. The cube follows the camera through `infiniteDistance`; an observer sizes the sky and stars for the camera's actual far plane. No collision, picking, navigation, shadows, environment cube, or camera controls are modified by the module.

Call `const solar = sky.update(time, weather)` each render update. The return object is reused; copy vectors/colors if retaining snapshots. `solar.sunDirection` points from the viewer toward the solar disk. Set `sun.direction.copyFrom(solar.sunDirection).negateInPlace()` and base solar intensity on `solar.daylight` (zero at night). A below-horizon sun must not be reused as positive-intensity moonlight. The existing ambient/environment/night-light systems remain separate. If using the new horizon palette for fog, copy `solar.horizonColor` to `scene.fogColor` after the existing Atmosphere update, which otherwise overwrites fog color. Call `sky.dispose()` at teardown.

## Cost and limits

The module has two meshes, two materials, 332 triangles, zero textures, zero physics bodies and zero render targets. Daytime renders the 12-triangle scattering background; night adds one 320-triangle additive star batch. Shader work covers visible sky pixels, so low geometry count does not establish a cheap GPU cost on every device. Uniform updates reuse state vectors/colors. Repeated weather/time updates and four create/dispose cycles retain stable resource and observer counts in the NullEngine test.

This first atmosphere pass has no cloud volumes, moving cloud shadows, moon or phases, astronomical constellations, lightning, altitude-dependent atmospheric density, or dynamic reflection capture. The native material applies its built-in tone curve; it should be compared visually with the game's surface exposure, rather than assuming it is a physically calibrated HDR light source. The world environment lighting remains a separate prefiltered texture. Dense rain appears hazier, but this is not a cloud or storm simulation.

## Verification

`tests/sky.test.ts` verifies a finite normalized solar vector, sunrise/sunset clock wrapping, bounded settings, shared light direction, night/rain star suppression, exact geometry budgets, stable resources and immediate observer cleanup. `scripts/sky/preview.html` and `preview.ts` provide an isolated rendered sky with simple scale/lighting reference objects. `tests/sky-preview.mjs` captures seven states on each renderer in Chrome at 1600 × 900; retained images and JSON are in `docs/evidence/sky/`. This preview is separate from the game and does not establish integrated performance, a 30-minute stability result, or final art/reference fidelity.

The completed 9 September 2026 preview has 14 stages and zero runtime/console errors. Day, sunset and night outputs were opened for visual inspection; the night pass contains visible stars and the sunset pass places the solar disk over the warm horizon. An initial preview-only favicon 404 was removed before the final capture. Scene counts remain nine meshes/four materials/two textures throughout the studio states (including scale objects and their shadow resources); Sky itself owns none of those textures. The recorded draw-call counter is cumulative across studio frames and must not be read as draw calls per frame or an integrated game cost.

## Integrated gameplay verification

The 9 September 2026 normal-game audit passed eight states on each backend, 16 total, with zero runtime/console errors. `tests/sky-gameplay-audit.mjs` starts a fresh game, uses normal map fast travel, the Creative panel's native keyboard-operated time slider and weather selector, then Creative flight and pointer-lock mouse look for a coastal skyline viewpoint. The test hooks only read loaded scene data. Captures cover street and coastal noon, 17:48 sunset, clear 23:00 night, 15:00 haze, 15:00 rain, rainy night and an upward clear-night star view. Evidence is retained in `docs/evidence/sky-gameplay-webgpu-coastal-v8/` and `docs/evidence/sky-gameplay-webgl-coastal-v8/`.

Both runs used Chrome 152.0.7977.83 at 1600 × 900 against the frozen `coastal-detail-v8` build on port 4198: main module `index-CxJrQY1k.js`, SHA-256 `0e304f177228f219ba9cc1c5ccad41347655f108c4deedb543f41583541e5dcc`; world `authored-860409-v6-dry-sand`, manifest SHA-256 `f09fd783847151550cd3aba36229e733fbeca0538cd8d15013d070263a6ae29b`. GPU browsers ran serially and closed before the next audit.

Read-only checks confirmed native WGSL on WebGPU and GLSL on WebGL2, compiled sky/star effects, a solar-vector/light-direction dot product of −1, zero directional sunlight at night, weather-driven scattering and fog, clear-night stars and rainy-night star suppression. Sky resources remained two meshes/two materials and 332 triangles in every state. The background is non-pickable, does not receive fog, disables depth writes, follows the camera, and precedes all active opaque world materials in its rendering group.

Opened day, sunset, night, haze and rain screenshots on both backends show the intended gradients, solar disk, dark night sky, visible stars and weather changes. The city remains visible in front of the background, including active structures approximately 710 m away, beyond the cube's 412.5 m half-size. Elevated captures also expose the existing finite terrain edges and the difference between the authored fog palette and sky horizon; these are outstanding world coverage/art limitations. This short functional audit does not establish performance, long-session stability, cloud simulation or reference parity, and required no runtime fix.
