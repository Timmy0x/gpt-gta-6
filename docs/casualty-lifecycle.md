# Casualty lifecycle and player recovery

The previous `resetPlayer` path called a global ragdoll reset followed by `population.reset()`. Three seconds after BUSTED or WASTED, this healed every civilian, moved them home and reconstructed all four facility guards. Save/load also reset ambient casualties, because the old save contained health only for creative civilians. This explains real resurrection after player recovery; it is separate from a surviving actor getting up after a nonfatal hit.

Ordinary player recovery now calls `combat.reactions.reset({preserveFatal:true})` and `population.reset(false)`. Pursuit and the local alarm clear, but civilian health/location and dead guard/officer state survive. Fatal ragdolls retain their settled bone pose when physical bodies are disposed. An explicit creative **Reset encounter** continues to call the full reset and intentionally revives actors. A nonfatal knockdown still blends back to movement after approximately 3.4 seconds, with its reduced health intact; it is not a respawn.

`Population.serializeCasualties()` and `restoreCasualties()` provide a versioned, validated ledger. It stores stable IDs and positions for up to 60 civilian casualties, the four authored guards, and the latest 24 response corpses, together with the next unique response-officer number. Save parsing rejects invalid coordinates, duplicate IDs, invalid guard/role names, oversized lists and an officer sequence that could reuse a saved identity. Creative civilian records are restored before the ledger is applied. Legacy creative saves containing health zero but no ledger acquire a dead pose during the next population update.

Saved corpses use a static lying pose rather than serializing the physical ragdoll constraint graph. Ambient and creative civilians are never automatically replaced by the population loop. Guards preserve health across range-based controller activation and do not respawn when revisiting the annex. Civilian/guard rigs remain bounded by the existing 60/4 actor budgets; fatal physics is released after settling.

Living response officers have a separate 12-actor dispatch limit. Dead actors do not count as living backup. Nearby visible casualties are retained; once a response body is older than 25 seconds, farther than 50 metres and outside the actual camera view, it can be disposed. This cleanup also runs when police response is disabled. A 24-corpse dispatch budget and hard 36 total-rig cap prevent repeated kill/recovery cycles from growing memory indefinitely. When visible corpses fill the budget, new dispatch waits for safe retirement. Retired identities are not reused; saving only the latest bounded corpse set never restores an older identity as a living actor.

## Verification

`tests/police-lifecycle.test.ts` uses actual Havok and the production population classes. It covers fatal civilian/guard/officer persistence through ordinary recovery, nonfatal health preservation, explicit revival, casualty save round trips, guard activation/deactivation, legacy health-zero saves and validation rejection. Forty repeated kill/recovery/dispatch cycles stay within the combined response-rig cap; distant cleanup with police disabled restores physics body count to baseline. A separate rig test checks role-colored trousers, hand-attached firearm position and a forward aimed barrel. These physical/geometry checks do not replace browser visual review.

`tests/casualty-controls-audit.mjs` independently passed 17 browser stages in Chrome WebGL2 at 1600 × 900 on frozen build `index-DTgddOO0.js`. A civilian spawned through the creative UI (`ped-30`) received ten actual aimed SMG shots, reached health zero and remained dead after 10.5 seconds. The normal one-star response produced BUSTED after 28 seconds and ordinary recovery by 32 seconds, including the $300 penalty. The same civilian stayed dead. Normal Save → Load → fresh page Continue retained that ID, zero health and a visibly lying corpse. The BUSTED and fresh-Continue screenshots were independently opened and inspected. No runtime errors or console warnings occurred; concurrent GPU work means no performance result is claimed. Evidence is in `docs/evidence/casualty-controls/result.json` and its adjacent PNGs.

The browser used only UI, keyboard and mouse inputs, with read-only diagnostics; it did not apply lethal damage through a test hook. WASTED uses the same corrected recovery function but was not separately exercised by this browser script. Officer/guard casualty and nonfatal-recovery cases are covered by the physical tests, not falsely included in this particular civilian browser result.

## Limits

There are no medical responder jobs, corpse investigation, body transport, persistent forensic clues or saved per-bone corpse poses. Temporary survivor panic/report timers are cleared by player recovery. The actor pool remains a small prototype population, and manually removing a civilian is an explicit world edit. Full casualty continuity across regional world regeneration, multiplayer sessions and very large crowds is outside this implementation.
