# Casualty lifecycle and player recovery

The previous `resetPlayer` path called a global ragdoll reset followed by `population.reset()`. Three seconds after BUSTED or WASTED, this healed every civilian, moved them home and reconstructed all four facility guards. Save/load also reset ambient casualties, because the old save contained health only for creative civilians. This explains real resurrection after player recovery; it is separate from a surviving actor getting up after a nonfatal hit.

Ordinary player recovery now calls `combat.reactions.reset({preserveFatal:true})` and `population.reset(false)`. Pursuit and the local alarm clear, but civilian health/location and dead guard/officer state survive. Fatal ragdolls retain their settled bone pose when physical bodies are disposed. An explicit creative **Reset encounter** continues to call the full reset and intentionally revives actors. The former universal 2.7-second fall plus 0.7-second recovery also made gunshot survivors appear to revive immediately. That behavior has now been replaced by the injury rules below.

`Population.serializeCasualties()` and `restoreCasualties()` provide a versioned, validated ledger. Version 2 stores stable IDs, positions, health, injury source and remaining recovery delay (null for incapacitation) for up to 60 civilian casualties, the four authored guards, and the latest 24 response casualties, together with the next unique response-officer number. Version 1 remains readable as fatal casualties. Save parsing rejects invalid coordinates, duplicate IDs, invalid guard/role names, oversized lists and an officer sequence that could reuse a saved identity. Creative civilian records are restored before the ledger is applied. Legacy creative saves containing health zero but no ledger acquire a dead pose during the next population update.

Saved dead and injured actors use a static lying pose rather than serializing the physical ragdoll constraint graph. Ambient and creative civilians are never automatically replaced by the population loop. Guards preserve health across range-based controller activation and do not respawn when revisiting the annex. Civilian/guard rigs remain bounded by the existing 60/4 actor budgets; fatal physics is released after settling.

Available response officers have a separate 12-actor dispatch limit. Down actors do not count as available backup. Nearby visible casualties are retained; once a down response actor is older than 25 seconds, farther than 50 metres and outside the actual camera view, it can be disposed. This cleanup also runs when police response is disabled. A 24-corpse dispatch budget and hard 36 total-rig cap prevent repeated kill/recovery cycles from growing memory indefinitely. When visible corpses fill the budget, new dispatch waits for safe retirement. Retired identities are not reused; saving only the latest bounded casualty set never restores an older identity as a living actor.

## Injury rules

These are explicit project gameplay defaults, not medical injury predictions or published GTA VI values. Health still determines death; incapacitated survivors retain positive health. There is no automatic healing or invented lethal damage to keep someone down.

- Every projectile hit incapacitates its victim for the encounter. This prototype does not yet distinguish a graze from a penetrating wound.
- An impact of at least 45 damage, an explosion of at least 20 damage, a fire tick of at least 20 damage, or remaining health of 35 or less also incapacitates.
- Recoverable blunt hits wait 10 seconds at damage 25 or less, or 18 seconds above 25, followed by a 2.2-second recovery blend. Further hits restart at least the appropriate delay; serious later injury cancels recovery.
- Ordinary BUSTED/WASTED recovery and normal Save/Load preserve dead and injured people. Explicit creative **Reset encounter** heals and revives them.
- Physical simulation runs for 6 seconds for survivors and 8 for fatalities. The eight-ragdoll budget settles older bodies in their current pose; it never makes a person healthy or upright. Injury timers and incapacity continue without Havok bodies. Subsequent hits can reactivate physics from the fallen pose, and settled visible meshes remain combat targets.
- Character locomotion and seated-pose entry points reject animation while down or dead, guarding against another system accidentally overwriting a casualty's pose.

`Character.injury` holds the source and remaining delay. `Combat` supplies damage context for bullets, melee, fire and blasts; vehicle impacts use the default impact source. `Population.onCharacterRestored` lets combat resume saved survivor timers without allocating a new ragdoll. The existing `preserveFatal` reset option now preserves all down actors; the name remains compatible with the main recovery call site.

## Limits

There is no medical treatment or autonomous recovery from severe incapacitation yet; explicit encounter reset is the available revival path. This injury model has no bleeding simulation, wound location, limp animation, or medical responder jobs, corpse investigation, body transport, persistent forensic clues or saved per-bone corpse poses. Temporary survivor panic/report timers are cleared by player recovery. The actor pool remains a small prototype population, and manually removing a civilian is an explicit world edit. Full casualty continuity across regional world regeneration, multiplayer sessions and very large crowds is outside this implementation.
